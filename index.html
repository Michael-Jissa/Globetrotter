Welcome

Lalibela was built in the late 12th and early 13th centuries during the reign of King Lalibela of the Zagwe dynasty, who set out to create a "New Jerusalem" for Ethiopian Christians. Instead of building upward with blocks, artisans carved 11 churches downward into volcanic rock, forming doors, windows, columns, and passageways from a single mass of stone. This monolithic style makes Lalibela one of the world's most extraordinary examples of medieval architecture, and the churches remain active places of worship today.
